San Diego Padres @ Milwaukee Brewers, 5:30p

Summary

Good news for anyone with nothing else to do tonight: this is NLDS Game 1, the Brewers' 103-win juggernaut against a Padres club that just swept the Wild Card, and the pitching matchup is as lopsided on paper as it gets. Misiorowski, baseball's hardest-throwing starter, enters off a season where he went 16-5 with a Major League-best 1.80 ERA and 252 strikeouts in 174 2/3 innings, and his gaudy 14.95 pNERD (fueled by a 54 xFIP- and 100.6 mph average velocity) backs up the hype — this is as close to must-see swing-and-miss stuff as the sport offers, with a Milwaukee has handled him carefully, capping him at three innings in his last regular-season outing adding a small subplot about workload. Robbie Ray, on the other end, is barely replacement-level by these numbers (0.63 pNERD), his command issues showing up in an ugly strike rate; reports note a left-hander issuing nearly six walks per nine against the best contact lineup in the Majors can lose a game in one inning, though San Diego's choice is a matchup play — Milwaukee hit .245 against lefties this season, one of the widest platoon gaps in baseball. Both lineups carry strong tNERD marks (Padres 10.85, Brewers 10.05) thanks to excellent bullpens and baserunning, pushing the overall gNERD to 18.24 — comfortably above today's slate average and firmly in the upper tier historically. Playoff stakes, a dominant young ace, and a flawed-but-savvy Padres counterpunch make this one worth your evening.

Chicago White Sox @ Cleveland Guardians, 10:00a

Summary

This is Game 1 of the 2026 ALDS, and that detail alone should bump this to the top of your watch list. The Cleveland Guardians, winners of their third straight AL Central title after climbing out of a 61-66 hole in August, host a Chicago White Sox club that finished just one game back and actually won the season series — the two sides split a surprisingly competitive year, with the White Sox up 7-6 in the season series after 13 meetings, and this marks the first-ever postseason meeting between two franchises that have shared a division since 1994. Both offenses arrive hot, with the Guardians going 8-2 in their last 10 with a .265 average while the White Sox went 8-2 as well, hitting .273 and outscoring opponents by 30 runs over that stretch.

On the mound, this is as good as low-key pitching matchups get: Hagen Smith enters at 3-3 with a 1.29 ERA, 0.96 WHIP and 41 strikeouts, while Parker Messick sits at 12-9 with a 2.56 ERA, 1.06 WHIP and 193 strikeouts. Smith's elite swinging-strike rate and tiny xFIP- give him the higher pNERD, with Messick's quick pace and youth making him plenty watchable too. With a gNERD of 17.33 — comfortably above both the historic 95th percentile (15.6) and near today's slate average — plus a stacked, young, deep-bullpen matchup on both sides, this is the postseason tilt to actually block time for.

Atlanta Braves @ Los Angeles Dodgers, 1:00p

Summary

Good enough info to write the summary.

NLDS Game 1, and the Dodgers are stacking the deck with a borrowed ace. Tarik Skubal — the two-time reigning AL Cy Young winner, acquired from Detroit at the deadline — makes his first career playoff start, and the Braves are sending out a bullpen game to try to survive it.

Skubal's pNERD of 11.87 is elite, built on a microscopic xFIP-, well above-average swinging-strike and strike rates, and upper-90s velocity — exactly what you want from a postseason ace. Atlanta, meanwhile, just activated Dylan Dodd from the injured list after spine inflammation, and he's expected to open before turning things over to the bullpen, with Dodd's pNERD unavailable since he's barely a real starter here. The Dodgers' tNERD (8.85) dwarfs Atlanta's (5.17), fueled by top-shelf batting runs, a dominant bullpen, and some positive regression luck still owed to their offense. Historically this 15.45 gNERD sits well above average, though it's actually below today's slate average of 16.13 — still, with a three-peat on the line, the Dodgers can take control of the series before facing one of Atlanta's best starters in Game 2. Worth watching, even if the pitching matchup looks lopsided on paper.

This is a genuine postseason heavyweight bout, and the gNERD score knows it. Gerrit Cole and Drew Rasmussen squaring off to open the ALDS gives you two legitimately nasty arms, a quietly loaded Rays lineup, and a Yankees team trying to avoid history repeating itself in St. Petersburg.
