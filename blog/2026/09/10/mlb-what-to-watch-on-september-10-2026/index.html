Across the mound, Hagen Smith makes his first career start after debuting on August 9 and pitching excellently in short bursts, owning a 0.56 ERA with 23 strikeouts across 16 innings in relief appearances. Worth tuning in for the young arm alone.

DeGrom sits at a 11.01 pNERD — among the best you'll see today — thanks to elite strikeout stuff and a sparkling xFIP-, and his surface-level numbers (3.96 ERA) are actually underselling him. The underlying numbers suggest that deGrom's struggles are mostly just unluckiness, with a sterling 2.69 FIP lately, which lines up with the positive Luck component in his profile pointing toward better days ahead. Gilbert is no slouch either (7.74 pNERD, mid-90s heat), though he's been homer-prone lately — an unusually high 4.68 ERA and matching 4.69 FIP over his last 10 starts, with a 10.6% opponent barrel rate. Seattle's offense is the drag here, ranking near the bottom of the league in scoring, which explains their sub-2 tNERD. Texas brings more thump and a well-above-expectations Luck bump. Wheeler carries the second-highest pNERD in today's slate, and it shows: he's coming off consecutive quality starts, having gone six innings against Atlanta and surrendered just one earned run on three hits, and his xFIP- of 75 suggests his shiny ERA isn't smoke and mirrors. On the other side, Cristian Javier brings a pNERD near replacement level after a season slowed by a shoulder issue; he's shown flashes—his last outing was solid—but the peripherals (rough strikeout rate, ugly road splits) keep his component scores in the red. The overall gNERD of 9.59 sits below the historic median and below today's average, so treat this as a solid Wheeler outing wrapped in an otherwise middling package rather than appointment viewing.

Tampa Bay Rays @ Atlanta Braves, 9:15a

Summary

Both teams enter this getaway-day matinee locked into playoff spots but far from must-win drama, and the on-paper NERD numbers say to keep expectations modest. Atlanta comes in having dropped three straight and has been outscored 14-3 by Tampa Bay through the first two games of this series, while the Rays are riding a 6-4 stretch and have already banked series wins on Junior Caminero's power surge. Nick Martinez has quietly been a top-15 pitcher this year at 14-4 with a 3.04 ERA and 95 strikeouts, and he's 2-1 with a 2.81 ERA in his career against Atlanta, though his modest pNERD reflects unspectacular strikeout stuff rather than dominant peripherals. Martín Pérez, by contrast, is coming off a rough outing where he tossed 3 2/3 innings, allowing four earned runs while giving up seven hits against the Phillies, and his diminished velocity caps his upside here. Atlanta's rotation depth is also thinning, with Robert Suárez and Joe Jiménez injured and Spencer Strider's 2026 return in doubt. With a gNERD of 8.32 sitting below both the historical and today's averages, this is a game for standings-watchers more than highlight-seekers.

Summary

This one's a mismatch on paper, and the numbers back it up: the Yankees are heavily favored, the Rockies are reeling, and only Max Fried's return from injury gives this game any pulse. Colorado's tNERD sits at a miserable 1.32, dragged down by weak batting runs and a barrel rate that ranks near the bottom of the league, while New York's 7.90 is buoyed by strong power numbers and a deep bullpen — the widest team gap you'll find on the slate. Fried, back from the injured list, has been sharp: dominant in his last five starts, allowing just four runs while striking out 26 batters in that span, and his 4.30 pNERD reflects strong underlying command, though his plodding pace knocks some shine off. With an 8.16 gNERD, this lands below both the historical and today's averages; save your remote for something else.
